Why do users add fashion products to their wishlist?
Key Findings
One retrieved review indicates users add items to their wishlist to keep them as a "maybe" for later consideration.
The evidence does not reveal other explicit motivations for wishlisting.
Users acknowledge that the wishlisting action itself is easy to perform.
Supporting Evidence
Only one out of three retrieved reviews explicitly explains the motivation for wishlisting.
The user states they "just keep things there as a maybe."
The remaining reviews discuss wishlist management without explaining initial add reasons.
Evidence Gaps
The sample lacks insights into whether users wishlist items for price tracking or outfit planning.
It remains unknown if this behavior reflects a broader trend or an isolated preference.
Confidence
Low. The retrieved sample contains only one explicit mention of wishlisting motivation, making it insufficient to establish a reliable pattern.

Users instead report forced delays and unexplained order cancellations after payment.

No evidence indicates users intentionally delay purchasing due to hesitation or comparison.
Supporting Evidence

One user states: "order gets delayed without any information on the day of delivery after confirming availability."
All five reviews focus on post-purchase fulfillment failures rather than pre-purchase decision-making.
Evidence Gaps
The sample lacks any mention of voluntary postponement or pre-purchase barriers.
User intent and timing of purchase delays remain unaddressed in the retrieved data.
Confidence
Low. The evidence exclusively documents post-purchase fulfillment failures, not voluntary postponement.
